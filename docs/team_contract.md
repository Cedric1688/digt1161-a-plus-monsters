Status: prepared for member confirmation. This draft consolidates the roles and working rules in the supplied Lab 4 team document with the course contract template. It does not assert that any member has signed or accepted it.

## 1. Shared goals

Deliver a small, documented Python code-review practice product, demonstrate clear teamwork and communication, and maintain a transparent record of contributions, decisions and reviews. Use fictional application data and distinguish plans from completed evidence.

## 3. Decision making

The card owner makes routine decisions and records them in its issue. New ideas enter the Backlog rather than being added silently to work in progress. Reprioritise at the two-week review. Changing MVP scope requires a majority; an urgent between-meeting scope change requires approval from three of four members. For a 2–2 tie, compare options against scope and acceptance criteria, run a small test if useful, and request TA guidance if unresolved.

## 4. Communication and meetings

MS Teams and email are the formal channels, as stated in the course contract template and current proposal. The Lab 4 excerpts use WhatsApp and Google Meet; the team must confirm this channel alignment and any TA-approved alternative before signing.

- Weekly written stand-up before the lab: finished work with issue link; next task; blockers.
- Weekly rotating Lab Brief: completed work and evidence, next work and owners, risks and TA questions.
- Live review, retrospective and backlog reprioritisation every two weeks, about 45 minutes. The Lab 4 document proposes Fridays within 16:00–18:00; all members must reconfirm this slot.
- Answer messages and blockers within 24 hours and PR-review requests within 72 hours.
- Store meeting notes in `/docs/meetings/weekX.md` and decisions in the relevant issue.

## 5. Tracking and Definition of Done

Use GitHub Projects columns Backlog → In progress → In review → Done. Each card links to an issue with acceptance criteria, owner and due date. Limit In progress to one card per person and In review to three cards for the team. Flag a card stuck for more than three days; split, reassign or reschedule it explicitly.

Done requires acceptance criteria passing, at least one teammate's PR approval before merge, no self-merge, relevant documentation updated, a usable 375 px UI where applicable, and tested RLS policies for relevant data changes. Record AI assistance accurately.

## 6. Conflict resolution and accountability

Raise concerns privately, early and with examples about the work. If unresolved after two days, arrange a team discussion rather than waiting for the next scheduled meeting. Decide technical disagreements using acceptance criteria or a small comparison test. Use the decision rules above when needed. Persistent contribution problems lasting two weeks or more can be escalated to the TA or instructor.

Notify the team early about absence, unavailable time or a likely missed deadline. Record a revised plan and owner rather than marking unfinished work Done. Do not invent another member's work, availability or consent.

## 7. Feedback and revision

Each two-week retrospective identifies one or two concrete improvements to try. Review workload balance, response times, blocked work and review delays. Update the contract with a short change log; all members review material changes.
